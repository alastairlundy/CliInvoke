# Decision Ledger — Process Configuration Shape

Topic: deepening candidate **F2** — Unify `ProcessConfiguration`'s two ctors and clarify the role of the ctor-availability subclass. Sessions recorded in this ledger refine the shape of `ProcessConfiguration`, the visibility of the 15-param ctor, and the wrapper subclass that lets `ProcessConfigurationBuilder` (in a different assembly) invoke the protected ctor. The wrapper is **kept** (renamed `BuilderProcessConfiguration` per D007) and the long-term direction for eliminating it is DEFERRED (D006).

Scope: decisions in this ledger affect `src/CliInvoke.Core/Primitives/ProcessConfiguration.cs`, `src/CliInvoke/Builders/ProcessConfigurationBuilder.cs`, `src/CliInvoke.Specializations/Configurations/CmdProcessConfiguration.cs`, `src/CliInvoke.Specializations/Configurations/PowershellProcessConfiguration.cs`, and the test files that mutate `TargetFilePath` after `Build()`.

Cross-references:
- Domain glossary: `CONTEXT.md` (notably the `Process Invocation Context` definition).
- File-path-resolver ADR: `docs/decisions/DECISIONS-CliInvoke-file-path-resolver-seam.md` (currently absent from the working tree; cited in `CONTEXT.md`).

### [D001] — shape of `ProcessConfiguration`

- **Resolved Answer**: "Since the process Invocation Context isn't currently implemented Option 1 has to remain the answer until Process Invocation Context's creation. At that point a pivot to Option 2 should be done."
- **Normalized Requirement**: `ProcessConfiguration` shall be **frozen at construction** for every property **except** `TargetFilePath`, which remains `{ get; set; }` and is documented as the "resolution slot" the file-path resolver writes to at execution time. The two ctors shall collapse into a single canonical shape (the current 15-param protected ctor, made the single source of truth); the public 3-param ctor shall delegate to it. `ProcessConfigurationWrapper` shall be deleted. A future pivot to Option 2 (fully immutable `ProcessConfiguration` plus a new `Process Invocation Context` type that carries the resolved path at runtime) is required once the context type is implemented.
- **Constraints**: The pivot to Option 2 is **deferred** until the `Process Invocation Context` type is implemented. The pivot is a hard prerequisite, not a stylistic choice: Option 1 is a temporary state. The two `ProcessInvokerTests` (`Invoker_WhiteSpaceFilePath_ShouldThrow` and `Invoker_EmptyFilePath_ShouldThrow`) keep their `config.TargetFilePath = "..."` mutation pattern under Option 1; they must be rewritten when the pivot to Option 2 happens. The `ExternalProcess.StartAsync` write to `Configuration.TargetFilePath` at `ExternalProcess.cs:151` is the legitimate user of the resolution slot; it stays. The `CmdProcessConfiguration` ctor's `base.TargetFilePath = TargetFilePath;` and the `PowershellProcessConfiguration` ctor's `TargetFilePath = filePath; base.TargetFilePath = TargetFilePath;` patterns stay — they pre-resolve the path and pass it to the base. `TargetFilePath` is the *only* mutable property; no other property gains or keeps a setter.

### [D002] — accessibility of the canonical 15-param ctor

- **Resolved Answer**: "Option 2 with a caveat. For potential future public ctors for ProcessConfiguration: A 2nd public ctor is acceptable as long as it isn't unwieldy like the 15 param ctor, and as long as correct ctor Resolution Priority is annotated to de-prioritize it over the 3 param default ctor."
- **Normalized Requirement**: The 15-param ctor shall be `internal`; the 3-param ctor shall be the only public ctor and the friendly default. The builder (which lives in `CliInvoke`, the same assembly family) shall be the only internal consumer of the 15-param ctor. A second public ctor is permitted in the future only if it (a) is materially smaller than the 15-param ctor, and (b) is annotated with the project's "ctor resolution priority" mechanism so overload resolution prefers the 3-param ctor in ambiguous calls.
- **Constraints**: The "ctor resolution priority" mechanism is implementation-defined. In .NET 9+ this maps to `System.Runtime.CompilerServices.OverloadResolutionPriorityAttribute`. The `Polyfill` package (already acknowledged in `README.md` for "simplifying older TFM support") backports this attribute to .NET 8.0 and earlier, so the project can apply the attribute on all three target frameworks without conditional compilation. .NET 8.0 support ends no later than November 2026; the polyfill is a short-lived bridge, not a permanent commitment. The "second public ctor" caveat is a forward constraint, not a present-tense requirement.

### [D003] — specializations' construction story under D002

- **Resolved Answer**: DEFERRED.
- **Normalized Requirement**: `CmdProcessConfiguration` and `PowershellProcessConfiguration` shall remain as `public` subclasses of `ProcessConfiguration` (the type hierarchy is preserved), but their constructors shall stop calling the internal 15-param ctor directly. Instead, they shall construct a `ProcessConfiguration` value through a public `ProcessConfiguration` factory or through the existing public `IProcessConfigurationBuilder` and use that as the source of truth for the field set. The specialization's class identity (its type, its `TargetFilePath` derivation, its platform attributes) is preserved; only the construction mechanism changes.
- **Constraints**: Branch deferred at user's request ("I'm going to need to revisit this later. For now it is deferred."). Two open sub-questions remain for the revisit: (1) whether the specializations use the existing `ProcessConfigurationFactory` in `CliInvoke` (requiring a `CliInvoke.Specializations` → `CliInvoke` dependency change) or the `IProcessConfigurationBuilder` from `CliInvoke.Core` (no dependency change); (2) the contradiction with D002 — the specializations' ctors still need to call the internal 15-param ctor to subclass `ProcessConfiguration`, so the public factory/builder alone is not sufficient; the revisit must pick one of `InternalsVisibleTo` from `CliInvoke.Core` to `CliInvoke.Specializations`, composition (the specialization wraps a `ProcessConfiguration` instead of inheriting), or promoting a public `Create` method on `ProcessConfiguration` (re-opens D002). The formal D002 rule about ctor resolution priority does not cover a static factory method, and the spirit of D002 (the friendly 3-param ctor is the preferred entry point) is not formally enforced on a static factory.

### [D004] — shape of `ProcessConfiguration` (re-opened)

- **Resolved Answer**: "Re-open D001 and D002 - The wrapper stays, needs a rename, and a mention somewhere to agents that will helpfully explain why this wrapper should not be deleted. A long term solution for how to eliminate the wrapper should be developed."
- **Normalized Requirement**: Same as D001 — `ProcessConfiguration` shall be **frozen at construction** for every property **except** `TargetFilePath`, which remains `{ get; set; }` and is the resolution slot. The two ctors shall collapse into a single canonical shape (the 15-param ctor remains the source of truth; the public 3-param ctor shall delegate to it). **The wrapper shall not be deleted** — it is the legitimate mechanism by which the builder (in `CliInvoke`, a different assembly) calls the `protected` 15-param ctor. The wrapper shall be **renamed** to a more self-documenting name. An **agent-facing comment** shall be added to the wrapper explaining (a) its purpose, (b) why it should not be deleted, and (c) that a long-term solution for eliminating the wrapper is being developed.
- **Constraints**: `Supersedes: D001`. The wrapper stays; D001's "wrapper shall be deleted" requirement is withdrawn. The 15-param ctor stays `protected` (see D005); no `InternalsVisibleTo` is needed. D003 (specializations' construction story) is **withdrawn as moot** because the `protected` 15-param ctor remains reachable from any subclass regardless of assembly, so the specializations can keep calling it directly with no extra plumbing. A **long-term solution for eliminating the wrapper shall be developed separately**; candidate directions include (a) the D001-deferred pivot to a fully-immutable `ProcessConfiguration` plus a new `Process Invocation Context` type that takes over the resolution slot, (b) a public static `Create` factory on `ProcessConfiguration` that wraps the ctor, or (c) a parameter-object pattern that collapses the 15 params into a single value. The chosen rename and the agent-facing comment text are tactical details recorded in a follow-up branch.

### [D005] — accessibility of the canonical 15-param ctor (re-opened)

- **Resolved Answer**: (Same as D004 — re-opened together with D001.) The 15-param ctor stays `protected`; no `InternalsVisibleTo` is needed; the wrapper provides the access path from `CliInvoke` to the protected ctor.
- **Normalized Requirement**: The 15-param ctor shall remain `protected`. The public 3-param ctor shall be the only public ctor and the friendly default, and it shall delegate to the 15-param ctor. No `InternalsVisibleTo` grant is added to `CliInvoke.Core`; the `InternalsVisibleTo` block in `src/CliInvoke.Core/CliInvoke.Core.csproj:42–44` shall remain commented out (it was commented out for a reason — the wrapper made it unnecessary, and that situation is preserved). The ctor-resolution-priority mechanism from the prior D002 is no longer required because no `internal` ctor exists to de-prioritize.
- **Constraints**: `Supersedes: D002`. D004 (the proposed branch on ctor-reachability via `InternalsVisibleTo` or alternatives) is **withdrawn as moot** because the wrapper dissolves the reachability problem. The "second public ctor" caveat from the prior D002 is also withdrawn; the 3-param ctor is the only public ctor. The Polyfill package's `OverloadResolutionPriorityAttribute` backport is no longer a relevant dependency for this decision.

### [D006] — long-term solution for eliminating the wrapper

- **Resolved Answer**: DEFERRED.
- **Normalized Requirement**: A long-term solution for eliminating the wrapper shall be developed separately. Candidate directions, recorded here for the future branch: (a) the D001-deferred pivot to a fully-immutable `ProcessConfiguration` plus a new `Process Invocation Context` type that takes over the resolution slot, which removes the ctor-availability problem the wrapper solves; (b) a public static `Create` factory on `ProcessConfiguration` that wraps the ctor, giving the builder a non-subclass construction path; (c) a parameter-object pattern that collapses the 15 params into a single value, simplifying both the ctor and the call site.
- **Constraints**: Deferred at user's request ("Defer the long term solution for eliminating the wrapper."). The wrapper stays in its renamed form (see D007) until a long-term direction is chosen. The chosen direction will re-open D001 and D004 with `Supersedes` lines.

### [D007] — rename of the wrapper

- **Resolved Answer**: "Option 1 it is" (referring to `BuilderProcessConfiguration`).
- **Normalized Requirement**: The wrapper class shall be renamed from `ProcessConfigurationWrapper` to `BuilderProcessConfiguration`. The rename touches the type declaration at `src/CliInvoke/Builders/ProcessConfigurationBuilder.cs:426–443` and the call site at `ProcessConfigurationBuilder.cs:408`. The class remains `internal` and continues to subclass `ProcessConfiguration` for the sole purpose of calling the `protected` 15-param ctor. The file path is unchanged (the class lives in `ProcessConfigurationBuilder.cs`, not a separate file).
- **Constraints**: None. The rename is a tactical find-and-replace; no other code references the type name. The agent-facing comment (named in D004 as a follow-up) is a separate decision and is not part of this record.

### [D008] — format of the agent-facing comment on the renamed wrapper

- **Resolved Answer**: "Option 1 - A Remarks block is more appropriate."
- **Normalized Requirement**: The wrapper class `BuilderProcessConfiguration` shall carry a single XML doc `<remarks>` block (not a `<summary>` extension, not an inline `// note:` block, not both) that explains the wrapper's purpose and the no-delete rule. The block is the agent-facing comment; it is the only comment on the class. The `<summary>` element is omitted or kept minimal (e.g., "An internal subclass of `ProcessConfiguration` used by `ProcessConfigurationBuilder` to invoke the protected multi-parameter constructor.").
- **Constraints**: None. The content of the `<remarks>` block is covered by D009 (scope) and D010 (cross-references).

### [D009] — content scope of the `<remarks>` block

- **Resolved Answer**: "Option 1 with a modification - A mention of a long term solution should be added but no references to any decision ledgers."
- **Normalized Requirement**: The `<remarks>` block on `BuilderProcessConfiguration` shall contain three elements: (1) the ctor-availability problem statement (the builder lives in a different assembly, the 15-param ctor is `protected`, the wrapper is the legitimate cross-assembly access path), (2) the no-delete rule ("do not delete this class without first choosing a long-term replacement"), and (3) a plain-language mention that a long-term solution to eliminate the wrapper is being developed. The remark shall **not** reference any decision-ledger record (no `Dxxx` ID, no ledger file name). The mention of the long-term solution stays in plain English so the remark is self-contained and survives any future ledger renames.
- **Constraints**: When the long-term direction is chosen (D006 currently DEFERRED), an ADR (or ADR-adjacent document) shall be created at that time; the ADR is the natural cross-reference target for the wrapper's remark at that point. The plain-language mention in the remark is the bridge until the ADR exists.

### [D010] — cross-references in the `<remarks>` block

### [D011] — shape of the 3-param friendly ctor

- **Resolved Answer**: "Option 1" (3 params, delegate to 15-param ctor).
- **Normalized Requirement**: The public 3-param ctor at `ProcessConfiguration.cs:29` shall remain at 3 params (`targetFilePath`, `arguments = ""`, `outputRedirection = true`) and shall delegate to the protected 15-param ctor via `: this(targetFilePath, arguments, redirectStandardInput: false, outputRedirection: outputRedirection)`. The 11 hardcoded values currently in the 3-param ctor's body become the 15-param ctor's `??` defaults (they already match). The 3-param ctor's body is empty; argument validation lives in the 15-param ctor at line 76. A future contributor adding more params to the friendly ctor is touching the friendly-default contract; pair the deepening with a code-review note that flags any new param on the public 3-param ctor as a regression against this decision. ### [D012] — `Arguments` and `OutputRedirection` setter visibility

- **Resolved Answer**: "Option 1 it is" (`{ get; }` only, setters removed).
- **Normalized Requirement**: The protected setters on `Arguments` (line 121) and `OutputRedirection` (line 168) shall be removed. Both properties become `{ get; }` only. The ctors continue to set the backing fields directly via direct field assignment in their bodies (which is permitted for `{ get; }` auto-properties — the ctor can write to the implicit backing field). The frozen-type contract from D004 is fully honoured: `TargetFilePath` is the only mutable property.
- **Constraints**: Confirmed by grep: no production, test, or benchmark code in the repository calls `.Arguments =` or `.OutputRedirection =`. The setters are dead surface area; their removal is a cleanup, not a behaviour change. If a future subclass legitimately needs to mutate either property post-construction, it adds a `protected set` back with a justifying comment — the addition is the signal, not the default. A future contributor adding a setter back without a justifying comment is touching the frozen-type contract.

### [T001] — D011's 3-param ctor validation preservation

- **Resolved Answer**: "Option 1 it is"
- **Normalized Requirement**: The `ArgumentNullException.ThrowIfNull(arguments);` check shall be moved from the public 3-param ctor's body to the protected 15-param ctor's body, placed immediately after the existing `ArgumentException.ThrowIfNullOrEmpty(targetFilePath);` check at line 76. After the move, the 3-param ctor's body is empty per D011; the check is reached via the 3-param ctor's `: this(targetFilePath, arguments, redirectStandardInput: false, outputRedirection: outputRedirection)` delegation.
- **Constraints**: **Cites**: D011, D001, D004, D005. The check preserves the current public API behavior: callers that pass `null` to the 3-param ctor still get an `ArgumentNullException` with paramName `arguments` at construction. The stack trace shifts by one frame (the 15-param ctor's frame replaces the 3-param ctor's frame); the exception type and paramName are unchanged. Direct subclasses (`CmdProcessConfiguration`, `PowershellProcessConfiguration`, `BuilderProcessConfiguration` after D007's rename) pass non-null `arguments`, so the check is a no-op for them — but the path through the 3-param ctor's delegation is the one that needs protection, and the check is reachable there. The `targetFilePath` check (line 76) stays first because `targetFilePath` is the primary key.

### [T002] — `BuilderProcessConfiguration`'s `outputRedirection` default

- **Resolved Answer**: "Option 2 - The Builder operates under the assumption that the default value is false to be of most benefit to the user using the builder."
- **Normalized Requirement**: The `BuilderProcessConfiguration` (currently `ProcessConfigurationWrapper`) ctor's `outputRedirection` parameter default at `ProcessConfigurationBuilder.cs:430` shall remain `false`. The default is deliberately divergent from the 15-param ctor's `outputRedirection = true` default (`ProcessConfiguration.cs:63`) because the builder operates under the assumption that the user-friendly default for builder-driven construction is `false` (output redirection off until the user explicitly enables it). The wrapper is `internal` and the builder is its only caller; the builder passes `_outputRedirection` explicitly at line 409, so the default is never used in production.
- **Constraints**: **Cites**: D007, D004, D005, D013. The `false` default is a deliberate builder-centric choice, not a bug to fix. A future contributor reading the wrapper must not "fix" the `false` default to match the 15-param ctor's `true` default — the divergence is by design. The other 11 wrapper defaults (lines 431-439) match the 15-param ctor's defaults; `outputRedirection` is the only intentional divergence and the rationale is recorded here. If the builder's user-facing default ever changes to match the 15-param ctor's `true`, the wrapper's default is updated in lockstep; the two are coupled by this decision, not by code.

### [T003] — D010's build config for cref warnings

- **Resolved Answer**: "Option 3"
- **Normalized Requirement**: `Directory.Build.props` shall be updated to add `<WarningsAsErrors>CS1574;CS1580;CS1581;CS1584;CS1658;CS1734;CS1762</WarningsAsErrors>` (or the equivalent cref warning codes), treating only cref-related warnings as errors. The wrapper's remark and any other cref usage across the project gets build-failure protection for broken crefs, without the broader disruption of global `<TreatWarningsAsErrors>`. The list applies project-wide; the wrapper's remark is the primary motivator, but the protection is not scoped to the wrapper.
- **Constraints**: **Cites**: D010, D008, D009. The targeted list of cref warning codes must be maintained; a new cref warning code introduced in a future C# version isn't caught until added to the list. Existing `#pragma warning disable` blocks are unaffected: CS0618 at `ProcessConfiguration.cs:91-93`, CS8602 at `ProcessConfiguration.cs:217, 233`, and CA1416 at `ProcessConfigurationBuilder.cs:18, 445` continue to suppress those specific warnings. The list of cref warning codes should be reviewed during .NET upgrades to catch any new codes the compiler adds. If the project later decides global treatment is warranted, this decision is superseded by adding `<TreatWarningsAsErrors>true</TreatWarningsAsErrors>` and removing (or keeping as redundant) the targeted list.

### [T004] — D008-D010's remarks block structure

- **Resolved Answer**: "Option 2 makes the most sense"
- **Normalized Requirement**: The wrapper class `BuilderProcessConfiguration` shall carry a single XML doc `<remarks>` block containing three `<para>` elements, one per D009 element: (1) ctor-availability problem statement, (2) no-delete rule ("do not delete this class without first choosing a long-term replacement"), (3) plain-language mention that a long-term solution to eliminate the wrapper is being developed. The `<see cref="ProcessConfigurationBuilder" />` cross-reference (per D010) shall be placed in the third `<para>`. The `<summary>` element is kept minimal (per D008), e.g., "An internal subclass of `ProcessConfiguration` used by `ProcessConfigurationBuilder` to invoke the protected multi-parameter constructor."
- **Constraints**: **Cites**: D008, D009, D010. Each `<para>` carries a distinct constraint from D009; removing a `<para>` weakens the no-delete rule or the long-term mention. The `<see cref>` is in the third `<para>` because that's the "long-term direction" element, and the cref points to the only consumer (D010). The XML doc generator renders each `<para>` as a separate paragraph in the output, matching the visual separation. The remarks block is the agent-facing comment; it is the only comment on the class. The plain-language mention of the long-term solution (per D009) stays in prose, not a cross-reference, so the remark survives any future ledger renames.
